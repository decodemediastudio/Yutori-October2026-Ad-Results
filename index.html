What we did

In July, the Kobido message drew more booking clicks than any other message on Google, and the Kobido & Hadado ad was one of the two ads that carried the Meta campaign. We recommended dedicated Kobido & Hadado pages and a focused Kobido campaign using the £400 Google Ads credit. This report covers that campaign.

For four weeks, Yutori's ads appeared on Google when people in London searched for facial treatments, and sent them to the new Kobido & Hadado page. We wanted to learn what words people use to find a treatment like Kobido, and whether visitors go on to book.

The main finding

People search for facial massage, and they respond to the Kobido offer. Nine out of ten clicks came from everyday phrases like "facial massage London", and almost half of those visitors clicked "Book" on the Kobido & Hadado page. The page already uses this language.

  • Kobido works as the offer, not as a search term. In July, the Kobido message drew more booking clicks than any other message on Google, which is why we recommended this campaign. Few people search for the word itself: the Kobido and Hadado ad group had 425 impressions in four weeks, and in July the "Kobido Facial London" headline drew only 11 clicks. So the campaign reached people through the searches they do use, such as "facial massage London", and showed them the Kobido offer and page. Almost half of them clicked "Book". The Kobido and Hadado ad group converted at a similar rate, 43%.
  • "Facial massage London" had a higher CTR than the other two groups. 11.56% means about 12 in every 100 people who saw this ad clicked it.
  • "Facial sculpting and Japanese face massage" reached a wide audience. It had 2,217 impressions, half of the campaign's total.

July compared with October

July advertised the whole clinic and sent people to a shared treatments page. October followed the July recommendation: it focused on Kobido and sent people to the new Kobido & Hadado page. It was paid for with the £400 Google Ads credit, spent less, and a far larger share of its visitors clicked "Book". Both campaigns counted the same conversion, a click on the "Book" button.

The trade-off

October reached fewer people, and each click cost more: £0.95 against £0.26, about 3.7 times as much. Because a larger share of visitors clicked "Book", the cost of each conversion rose by 41p, about 24%. October brought fewer conversions in total, 176 against 238.

What this shows

Jane recorded about two unfinished online Kobido bookings for every one completed. This suggests some people start booking online but stop partway, so the online booking process is worth reviewing.

These bookings can't be attributed to the ads.

Next steps

  1. Apply the same approach to other treatment pages. The Kobido & Hadado page already leads with the words people search for. Upcoming treatment pages will follow the same method: use the everyday phrases people type into Google, then explain Yutori's approach.
  2. Finalise booking tracking. A booking conversion is already set up in Google Ads. The next step is to confirm it records completed Jane bookings and make it the main conversion, so future campaigns report appointments, not only clicks on "Book".
  3. Find out why Kobido bookings were left unfinished. Go through the Jane online booking steps for Kobido as a new client would, on a phone, and check for anything that could stop someone, such as price display, account sign-up or payment. In July, 96% of ad clicks came from mobile phones.
  4. Make booking Kobido clearer. Jane currently lists three separate Kobido services, each with a different practitioner or length. Clearer names, prices and lengths, and a "Book" button that goes straight to Kobido, may make it easier for people to finish booking.
  5. Pause the ads for now. The credit has been used. Our focus moves to helping people find Yutori through regular Google searches. Ads are worth trying again once booking tracking is confirmed.
